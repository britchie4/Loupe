    How it works

    Point
    Open Loupe and pick the app you're working on. As you move over it, Loupe highlights whatever is under your pointer and tells you what it is.
    Leave a note
    Click the thing you want changed and write the change in your own words. The note gets a numbered badge so you can see where it is.
    Hand it off
    Tell your agent “Check my Loupe notes”. It reads them, asks you on the note if something isn't clear, makes the change in your code, and turns the badge green.
    Or copy
    If you haven't connected an agent, click Copy and paste your notes into whichever coding agent you use.

    What your agent can and can't do

    Agent Link is off until you turn it on in Loupe's Settings. Once it's on, your agent can read the notes you leave and look at the app you picked, so it can check its own work. It can't click or type in any of your apps, and all of this stays on your Mac.

    Get Loupe

    Free and open source (MIT). Works on macOS 14 and later.

    Works with Claude Code, Codex, Cursor and Xcode's agents. Loupe walks you through connecting yours the first time you open it.

    Download .dmg Build from source

    Permission. The first time you open Loupe, it asks for permission to read other apps. macOS 27 and later call this “Device Control and Data Access”, and earlier versions call it “Accessibility”.

    Updating. If you're on 1.2.1 or later, Loupe updates itself. Choose Check for Updates… from the menu bar icon, or right-click the toolbar. If you're on an older version, quit Loupe, download the latest one, and drag it to Applications. You won't have to grant the permission again.
